# Lowest Quality, Spam and Untrustworthy Pages (QRG Section 4)

Contents: 1. Screening order · 2. Lowest triggers table · 3. Harmful · 4. Harmfully misleading · 5. Untrustworthy & deceptive · 6. Obstructed MC · 7. Malicious · 8. Spam policies · 9. Copied / paraphrased / AI content · 10. Detection method

## 1. Screening order (QRG 3.0, 4.0)

1. What is the true purpose? Harmful or deceptive purpose (including about who is responsible) → Lowest.
2. Could the page cause harm, is it untrustworthy, or spammy? → Lowest.
3. Only then rate how well the page achieves its purpose.

All page types get screened - government, university, big brands and charities too. Pages that *look* official or scientific can still be harmful.

## 2. Any one of these = Lowest (QRG 4.0 table)

| Area | Lowest trigger |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Harmful purpose; designed to deceive about purpose or ownership; made to benefit the owner (e.g. money) with little/no attempt to help visitors |
| MC quality | Harmful to self/others/groups; harmfully misleading; hacked/defaced/spammed; gibberish; little-to-no effort + no originality + no added value vs similar pages; so little effort/skill it fails its purpose |
| Site / creator info | Complete lack of info about who is responsible, on YMYL pages or pages needing trust |
| Reputation | Very negative reputation (fraud, malicious or criminal behaviour) |
| E-E-A-T | Highly untrustworthy; lack of E-E-A-T makes the page fail its purpose (highly inexpert YMYL page) |
| Ads / SC | MC deliberately obstructed by ads, SC, interstitials, download links that benefit the owner |
| Title | Extremely misleading, shocking or exaggerated |

Controversial, one-sided or distasteful content is not automatically Lowest - apply the criteria, not personal opinion.

## 3. Harmful content (4.2, 4.3)

- Harmful to self/others: incitement, threats, doxxing, easy-to-replicate violence instructions, glorifying atrocities, encouraging dangerous behaviour or downplaying risks, pro-suicide/pro-anorexia, health advice contradicting expert consensus that could cause serious harm or stop life-saving treatment. Judge against the most vulnerable reasonable viewer.
- Not harmful: news, fiction, education, scam-awareness pages, risky stunts shown with clear warnings.
- Harmful to Specified Groups (age, caste, disability, ethnicity, gender identity, immigration status, nationality, race, religion, sex, sexual orientation, veteran status, victims of violent events, other marginalized groups): promoting violence, intolerance, superiority/inferiority, dehumanizing stereotypes - when serious or mean-spirited. Criticism of ideas, education, news, history and non-mean comedy are not.

## 4. Harmfully misleading information (4.4)

Three types:
- Clearly inaccurate, refutable by widely accepted facts (false election dates, false death of a leader)
- Contradicts well-established expert consensus (lemons cure cancer; lottery as retirement saving)
- Unsubstantiated theories not grounded in evidence, especially eroding trust in institutions

Not counted: clear entertainment/satire/astrology/folklore with no factual claims; personal opinion reviews; genuinely debatable issues; trivial errors on trivial topics. A harmfully misleading **title** or a creator **misrepresenting medical credentials** can make the page Lowest even if the body is fine.

SEO relevance: health, supplement, finance, legal and product-claim content. Verify every consequential claim against authoritative sources (health ministry, WHO, regulators, peer-reviewed research). Unverifiable improbable claims = unsubstantiated.

## 5. Untrustworthy and deceptive (4.5.1 - 4.5.3)

Untrustworthy signals:
- Multiple or significant factual errors on an informational page
- Inadequate info about site/creator for the purpose - any site handling personal data, passwords or payments needs extensive contact info; YMYL/sensitive-data sites with no info at all = Lowest
- Lowest E-E-A-T or lowest reputation
- Deceptive purpose, design or intent
- Deliberately obstructed MC
- Scam, phishing, malware characteristics; asking for personal data without reason
- Manipulating people into actions that harm them for the site's benefit

Deception types (any = Lowest):
- **Deceptive purpose**: fake "independent review" or false claims of personal testing to drive affiliate clicks; product recommendations on a site impersonating a celebrity blog
- **Deceptive info about site/creator**: impersonating another brand (copied logo, look-alike URL); fake news site pushing an agenda; fake celebrity site with fake testimonials; claiming a physical store that doesn't exist (fake photo/address); **AI-generated or invented author profiles/photos to make content look human-written**; false credentials (e.g. fake doctor)
- **Deceptive design**: ads disguised as MC (fake search result pages), ads disguised as navigation (fake directories), buttons that do something unexpected (close → download app), title unrelated to content

## 6. Deliberately obstructed or obscured MC (4.5.4)

Lowest: ads covering MC while scrolling that can't be closed without clicking them; pop-ups closable only via an action that benefits the site; interstitials coercing downloads/clicks; ads pushing MC so far down users miss it; white-on-white or unreadable MC.
Not Lowest: paywalls, logins, geo-restrictions, content that failed to load. One easy-to-close pop-up is merely annoying.

## 7. Suspected malicious behaviour (4.5.5)

Strongly suspected scams, unjustified requests for personal data, phishing, malware links, browser malware warnings → Lowest without needing proof. In an audit of the user's own site, these usually indicate a hack - flag as urgent.

## 8. Spam policies (4.6)

| Spam type | What it looks like | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| No/little/gibberish MC (4.6.1) | Purpose can't be determined; deliberately thin pages; keyword pages that make no sense | Read the MC; sample sibling pages |
| Hacked, defaced, spammed (4.6.2) | Injected unrelated content/links; forum or comment sections full of bot link-spam | Look for off-topic sections, casino/pharma links, spam comments |
| Expired domain abuse (4.6.3) | Old trusted domain (school, charity, government) now hosting affiliate, casino, payday loan, medical product content | Compare current content vs Wayback Machine history |
| Site reputation abuse (4.6.4) | Third-party content (freelancer, white-label, partner) placed on a strong host mainly to borrow its ranking signals: "best casinos" on a medical site, payday-loan reviews on an education site, coupons from a white-label service on a news site, first-party site expanding into unrelated area mostly with freelance content | Is the section off-topic for the host? Who produces it? Is it distributed elsewhere? |
| Scaled content abuse (4.6.5) | Many pages made mainly for the owner with little value: mass AI or automated pages, scraped/synonymized/auto-translated content, stitched content, network of sites to hide scale, keyword pages that don't read sensibly | Look at several pages; same template + little unique value = scaled. Method of creation doesn't matter |
| Little/no effort, originality, added value (4.6.6) | All or almost all MC copied, paraphrased, embedded, auto/AI generated or reposted with no added value - even if credited | See section 9-10 |

NOT site reputation abuse: wire/press release services, syndicated news, UGC forums/comments, editorial columns, advertorials meant for readers (not rankings), properly marked affiliate links, coupons sourced directly from merchants.

## 9. Copied, paraphrased, embedded, AI content (4.6.6, 5.2.1)

- **Lowest**: all or almost all MC copied/paraphrased/embedded/reposted/AI-generated with little-to-no effort, originality and added value.
- **Low**: much of the MC is repackaged with only a little curation, editing or original input.
- Forms of copying: exact copy; copy with minimal alteration (word swaps, cropped images, clipped videos); copies of changing sources (SERPs, feeds); paraphrase from one or many sources (by people or AI).
- Not copying: licensed or syndicated content. Paraphrasing *can* add value (an expert explaining a regulation in plain language).
- AI: tool use alone doesn't set the rating. Telltale signs of unedited AI: leftover phrases like "As an AI language model / I don't have real-time data", generic summaries with no specifics, terms of use admitting auto-generated content.

## 10. How to detect copied/paraphrased content (4.6.7)

1. Pick distinctive sentences (avoid punctuation and odd words).
2. Search them in quotes and without quotes.
3. Compare matches: is most MC the same? Which is the authoritative/original source? Which has the earliest date (Wayback Machine)?
4. Paraphrase signs: only commonly known facts; heavy overlap with Wikipedia/reference sites; reads like a summary of one forum thread or news article; AI-assistant phrases.

For a pre-publish draft: run step 1-2 on 3-5 sentences, and ask "what does this page offer that the top 5 results don't?" If the answer is nothing, flag it.
