# E-E-A-T, YMYL and Reputation (QRG 2.3, 2.5, 3.3, 3.4)

Contents: 1. YMYL · 2. Identifying site owner & creator · 3. Contact & customer service · 4. E-E-A-T · 5. Experience vs expertise on YMYL · 6. Reputation research · 7. E-E-A-T audit checklist

## 1. YMYL - Your Money or Your Life (2.3)

A topic is YMYL if it could significantly affect people's health, financial stability, safety, or society's welfare - either because the topic is dangerous itself (self-harm, crime, extremism) or because inaccurate content could cause harm (heart attack symptoms, investing, earthquake response, voting eligibility, licence requirements).

Types: **Health & Safety** · **Financial Security** · **Government, Civics & Society** (elections, public institutions) · **Other**.

YMYL is a spectrum. Examples:

| Clear YMYL | May be YMYL | Not / unlikely |
|---|---|---|
| Tsunami evacuation routes | - | Weather forecast |
| When to go to the ER | How often to replace a toothbrush | How often to wash jeans |
| Opinion that a racial group is inferior | Opinion that an exercise is inferior | Opinion that a band is inferior |
| News on ongoing violence | News about a car accident | Local school sports news |
| Dangerous social challenge (Tide pods) | Hot sauce challenge | Music video |
| Buying prescription drugs | Car review | Buying pencils |

Two-question test:
1. Would a careful person seek experts or highly trusted sources? Could even minor errors cause harm? → likely YMYL.
2. Would most people be fine asking friends casually? → likely not YMYL.

Implication for SEO: clear-YMYL pages (supplements, medical devices, air purifier health claims, loans, insurance, legal, tax) get the strictest standard on accuracy, expertise, site transparency and claims.

## 2. Who is responsible and who created the MC (2.5.2)

It should be clear who owns the site and who made the content. Typical creators:
- Business homepage / product page: the website itself is responsible (even if an agency built it); user reviews can be important MC.
- Articles: named journalists, scientists or authors (or editorial board).
- Social profiles/channels: the account holder.
- Forums/Q&A: the posters (aliases acceptable).

## 3. About, contact, customer service (2.5.3)

- Amount needed depends on the site. Sites handling money (shops, banks) need extensive contact + policies on payment, exchange, returns, shipping.
- Humor/hobby/personal sites need less; legitimate anonymity is acceptable for personal sites and UGC.
- Look on homepage, Contact, Customer service, corporate site, official social pages. Local conventions matter.

## 4. E-E-A-T (3.4)

**Trust** is the core: is the page accurate, honest, safe, reliable? The trust needed depends on the page: secure payments and good service for shops; honest reviews meant to help buyers (not only sell); accuracy for YMYL information; low for harmless entertainment.

Supporting dimensions:
- **Experience**: first-hand or life experience (review by someone who used the product)
- **Expertise**: knowledge or skill (rewiring advice from an electrician)
- **Authoritativeness**: known as the go-to source (official government page for passports; a local business profile for current offers)

Evidence sources:
1. What the site/creator says about itself (About, author bio) - starting point only
2. What independent sources say (reviews, news, references) - wins when it conflicts with self-description
3. What's visible in the MC and comments (skill shown in the content, commenters vouching)

Conflicts of interest reduce trust: manufacturer "reviews", paid influencer promotions. Other trust factors: customer service for stores, peer review for academics. An untrustworthy page has low E-E-A-T regardless of how expert it looks.

Expertise vs experience (FAQ): expertise is often objective and testable; experience is subjective and narrated. Informal expertise counts. Alternative fields still have experts vs non-experts.

## 5. YMYL: experience or expertise? (3.4.1)

Personal experience on YMYL can be high E-E-A-T if trustworthy, safe and consistent with expert consensus. Factual information and advice must come from experts.

| Topic | Valuable experience | Leave to experts |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep in pregnancy | Non-medical comfort tips from people who lived it | Which sleep medications are safe |
| Liver cancer | Respectful forum on coping with treatment | Treatment options and life expectancy |
| Taxes | Humorous video about tax frustration | How to fill in tax forms |
| Retirement | Reviews of savings services by users | How much/where to invest |
| Voting | Personal post on why voting matters | Eligibility and registration |

Inaccurate YMYL facts inside an experience post (e.g. wrong voting date) → Lowest.

## 6. Reputation research (3.3)

Required for every audit with a live site. Research for the topic of the page (a site can be great for humour and untrustworthy for finance). Research the underlying company/creator across all their platforms.

Search patterns (replace brand/domain):
- `[brand -site:domain.com]`
- `["domain.com" -site:domain.com]`
- `[brand reviews -site:domain.com]`
- `["domain.com" reviews -site:domain.com]`
- `[brand site:wikipedia.org]`, news articles, awards, regulator notices, watchdog sites

Rules:
- Prefer independent, human-written sources; ignore traffic stats (Similarweb etc.) - not reputation.
- YMYL reputation should come from experts in the field (professional societies, recognized institutions).
- Customer reviews: be skeptical of both positive and negative; look at many; read content not just stars. Credible fraud reports = extremely negative; one rude clerk or late parcel ≠ negative reputation.
- Creators: degrees, peer validation, co-authors, citations, employment history; for influencers, bios/news/peer comments; for ordinary users, comments on the page.
- No reputation info for small sites or ordinary people is **neutral** - then weigh other factors more, especially on YMYL.

## 7. E-E-A-T audit checklist (for the report)

- [ ] Clear owner/company identity (legal name, address, registration where expected locally)
- [ ] Contact methods proportionate to purpose; customer-service policies for stores
- [ ] Named author on informational/YMYL content, with bio relevant to *this* topic
- [ ] Author credentials verifiable externally (LinkedIn, professional register, publications); no exaggeration
- [ ] No AI-generated fake author personas or stock photos posing as authors
- [ ] First-hand experience visible in MC (original photos, test data, specific details only a user would know)
- [ ] Claims cited to authoritative sources; consistent with expert consensus; dates current
- [ ] Reviews/affiliate content disclose relationships; not written solely to sell
- [ ] Independent reputation positive or neutral for this topic; no fraud/scam reports
- [ ] For YMYL: medical/financial/legal review by a qualified person where appropriate
