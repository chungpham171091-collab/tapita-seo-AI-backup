# Claim rewrite library

Solved problems. Reuse these patterns rather than inventing new phrasing under time pressure - each left-hand column is a claim that has caused, or would cause, a defect in at least one Coway market.

The rewrites are not euphemisms. They are accurate descriptions of what the product does, which is exactly why they are safe.

## Claim drift - the strengthening trap

The most common CRITICAL defect in localisation is not a mistranslation. It is a qualified English claim arriving in the target language as an absolute one. Watch the verb.

| Approved source intent | Must not become |
|---|---|
| Helps reduce airborne particles | Eliminates harmful particles / guarantees clean air |
| Designed to support cleaner indoor air | Protects your health / makes indoor air safe |
| Can help reduce particles associated with allergies | Relieves allergy symptoms / prevents allergies |
| Tested under stated laboratory conditions | Always achieves the same result in every home |
| Helps reduce certain odours and VOCs | Removes all gases and odours |
| Captures particles down to 0.01 µm | Kills viruses and bacteria |

Verbs to audit every time: helps, supports, reduces, captures, versus removes, eliminates, protects, prevents, guarantees, relieves. "Removes" and "captures" are acceptable when tied to a verified figure and its test conditions ("removes 99.999% of airborne particles down to 0.01 µm, laboratory results"); on their own, or applied to "all" pollutants, they are strengthened claims. If the target language has no neat equivalent for the qualified form, preserve the claim strength and escalate; never make the promise stronger because it reads better.

## Sleep and wellbeing

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Air purifiers help you fall asleep faster | An air purifier can help reduce airborne particles such as pollen, dust and PM2.5 in the bedroom, helping create a cleaner indoor environment for sleep |
| Reduces night-time awakenings | Runs at [X] dB in sleep mode, quiet enough to leave on overnight |
| Improves restorative sleep | Filters the air continuously overnight, when bedroom windows are usually closed |
| Ensures undisturbed rest / uninterrupted sleep | Operates at [X] dB in Sleep Mode, with indicator lights that can be switched off completely |
| For the best sleep we all dream of | Quiet enough for a bedroom at [X] dB, with a night light and display that can be toggled |
| Reduces morning congestion | *Symptom claim - remove; replace with an air-quality statement* |
| Supports better recovery and energy levels | *Remove entirely* |
| A commitment to your well-being | *Remove entirely* |

The pattern: swap the promised human outcome for the measurable machine behaviour - decibels, coverage in m², filtration, hours of operation. "Ensures undisturbed rest" is a promise about the sleeper; "20 dB in Sleep Mode with all lights off" is a fact about the product, and it sells the bedroom purifier better.

## Allergies, certifications and named conditions

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Ideal for asthma and allergy sufferers | Suitable for bedrooms where dust and pollen are a concern |
| Relieves hay fever symptoms | Helps reduce airborne pollen indoors during pollen season |
| Dual-certified allergy relief | Certified by ECARF and BAF; helps reduce airborne allergens such as pollen, dust and pet dander |
| ECARF-certified: reduces airborne allergens for easier breathing | ECARF-certified; helps reduce airborne allergens such as pollen, dust and pet dander |
| Helps reduce exposure to triggers for improved breathing comfort | Helps reduce airborne pollen, dust and pet dander in the room |
| Helps children with allergies breathe easier | Helps reduce airborne dust and pollen in children's bedrooms |
| Air purifier for allergy sufferers, asthma [as SEO keywords] | Air purifier for pollen and dust; HEPA air purifier for bedroom |

"Allergen" as a *particle category* is acceptable - it describes what is in the air. "Allergy sufferer", "symptoms", "trigger", "relief" and "easier breathing" describe a person and a condition, and are not. Keep the noun on the particle side of that line.

A certification certifies exactly what it certifies. ECARF and BAF/Allergy UK recognise allergy-friendliness and particle capture. Neither certifies relief, symptom improvement or easier breathing, so neither may be cited as evidence for those.

## Viruses, bacteria and infection

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Removes fine dust, allergens, mould and viruses | Removes 99.999% of airborne particles down to 0.01 µm, including dust, pollen, smoke and mould spores |
| Removes 99.9% of bacteria and viruses | *State the particle size instead. 0.01 µm is smaller than a virus - the specification implies the capability* |
| Protects your family from infection | *Remove. Direct disease-prevention claim in all four markets* |
| Antibacterial filter keeps your home healthy | Filter-hygiene treatment on the filter surface (tested to [standard], [lab]) - a claim about the filter, not the room; do not name the microorganism |
| Essential for flu season | *Remove. Names a disease and adds seasonal fear pressure* |
| Helping you breathe safer air every day | Helps reduce airborne particles and VOCs in everyday indoor environments |

The rule in one line: **state the particle size, never name the pathogen.**

## Filter terminology

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| True HEPA filter | HEPA filter, or HyperCaptive Filtration System where the system-level figure is meant |
| GreenHEPA / Green HEPA | HyperCaptive Filtration System |
| H13 HEPA filter | HEPA filter (drop the classification) |
| Medical-grade HEPA | HEPA filtration - "medical grade" implies a clinical status Coway does not claim |
| Unlike HEPA-type filters... | *Do not raise the comparison at all* |
| HEPA filtration: 99.999% down to 0.01 µm | HyperCaptive Filtration: removes 99.999% of airborne particles down to 0.01 µm |
| 3-in-1 filtration with a True HEPA filter | 3-stage HyperCaptive Filtration: vacuumable pre-filter, activated carbon and HEPA filter |

HEPA describes the media (more than 99.97% at 0.3 micron, single-filter performance). HyperCaptive describes Coway's system (99.999% down to 0.01 micron, product performance). Attributing the system figure to the media is both a terminology defect and an unsupported claim.

## Fear framing

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Indoor air is up to 5x more polluted than outdoor air - and it's making you ill | Indoor air can contain higher concentrations of some particles than outdoor air ([source]) |
| The vulnerable hours while you sleep | Overnight, when windows are usually closed and the room is not ventilated |
| Silently sabotages your sleep | Bedroom air quality is easy to overlook, particularly in rooms that stay closed overnight |
| The real culprit behind your restless nights | *Remove - fear framing plus an implied health claim* |
| Don't put your family's health at risk | *Remove entirely* |
| Buy before conditions worsen / act now | *Remove entirely* |

Test for fear: does the sentence describe a threat to a person, or a property of the air? Only the second is available.

## Numbers and efficacy

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Removes 99.9% of airborne particles | Removes 99.999% of airborne particles down to 0.01 µm (tested by [lab], report no. [ref], model [X]). Laboratory results; performance at home varies with room size, ventilation and use |
| Up to 99% cleaner air | *Meaningless without a defined measure. Replace with CADR* |
| Cleans a room in minutes | CADR [X] m³/h; cleans a [Y] m² room in [Z] minutes (based on a 2.4 m ceiling height) |
| Rapidly cleans large open spaces up to 109 m² in just minutes | CADR 421 m³/h; rated for rooms up to 109 m², and cleans 43 m² in about 15 minutes |
| Removes over 99% of VOCs and harmful fumes | Helps reduce VOCs including ammonia (NH₃) and acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO) - [X]% under [standard] test conditions, [lab] |
| Clinically proven | *Remove unless a clinical trial genuinely exists and the product is registered accordingly* |
| 100% ozone-free, perfectly safe for nurseries and sensitive individuals | Models with no ioniser: mechanical filtration only - no UV-C lamp and no ioniser, so the unit does not produce ozone. **Not for Airmega Mighty / C-Mighty**, which have an optional ioniser: write "optional ioniser that can be switched off", and state ozone results only with [standard, lab, report no.] |

Missing source? Mark it `[SUBSTANTIATION NEEDED: lab, report no., model, test conditions]` and list it. Never invent a lab, a report number or a standard.

## Absolute advice

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| Always leave a 3-foot clearance around the unit | Coway recommends leaving space around the intake and outlet - check the clearance stated in your model's manual |
| Elevated placement is always more effective | Raising the unit off the floor can help in some rooms; the [model] manual advises [X] |
| Never place an air purifier in a corner | A position with clear airflow on the intake side generally works better than an enclosed corner |
| Run it 24/7 for best results | Continuous operation on a low setting is a common approach; room size and filter life determine what makes sense |
| The optimal distance from the bed is 6-10 feet | Placing the unit within a couple of metres of the bed is common; follow the clearance guidance in the manual |

Anything presented as a rule needs a source: "Coway recommends", "the manual advises", or hedged general practice. This is a credibility issue as much as a compliance one - a reader who checks the manual and finds nothing stops trusting the article.

## Third-party statistics

Safe construction - context and product kept apart, no causal bridge:

> The WHO's air quality guidelines set an annual mean limit of [X] µg/m³ for PM2.5 [source, date].
>
> [New paragraph, no "which is why..."]
>
> When choosing a purifier for a bedroom, the specifications that matter most are CADR and the room size it is rated for.

Unsafe construction - a legal statistic plus a legal claim producing an illegal implication:

> ANSES reports that indoor air contains [X]. That's why a Coway purifier is essential in every French home.

The join is the violation, particularly in France. Cite for context, never as a premise the product concludes.

|---|---|
| Sleep better tonight with Coway | Find a Coway purifier rated for your bedroom size |
| Protect your family's health | Compare coverage, noise level and filter type across the range |
| Breathe easier - shop now | See which models are rated for rooms up to [X] m² |
| Bring pure air home with Coway today | Explore the Airmega range |
| Choose the Airmega 150 for a healthier home and a more peaceful life | Choose the Airmega 150 for rooms up to 73 m², at 20 dB in Sleep Mode |

CTAs are among the most-read lines in any asset and the most frequently skipped in claim review. Audit them as carefully as body copy.
