# Product Corrections Registry

Every product below was misdescribed in the 2026 audit batch. The "Verified" column is what the live TMF listing or the manufacturer actually says, as of 27 August 2026.

**Check this file before writing about any of these products.** Then still build the Fact Sheet row - prices, stock and review counts move, and this registry does not track them.

When you verify a new product and find the copy was wrong, add a row.

## Claims that were right and are worth reusing

The audit confirmed these as defensible. They are TMF's genuine information-gain assets.

- Airflow restriction at fittings: mismatched cuff diameter creates a choke point; match cuff to both hose ID and tool inlet; long runs amplify any leak. Supported by the Flash Cuff listing's own "inside diameter of the cuff is the same as that of the hose, keeping turbulence to a minimum".
- The palm-block leak test: block the wand end and feel for air escaping at each joint while the machine runs.
- Rotary jet extractors are airflow-calibrated, so CFM compatibility matters more than PSI. Confirmed by Rotovac's own listings.
- Rotary jet vs rotary scrubber vs CRB: only the jet extractor extracts; CRBs need a dry-vacuum pass.
- Natural stone: acid sensitivity, pH-neutral for polished stone, avoid acid unless the stone is confirmed tolerant, test inconspicuously.
- LVP: locking joints trap moisture, the wear layer dulls with harsh alkaline or abrasive pads.
- Wood floor finish protection: low moisture with immediate recovery, white or red pads for maintenance, never a black stripping pad on finished wood, pH-neutral chemistry.
- Residue as a soil magnet: surfactant film left in the pile attracts dry soil and the carpet looks dirty within days.
- Powder vs liquid trade-offs: powder holds up better in van heat, liquid can partially freeze in winter, powder is more concentrated per unit weight, liquid saves mixing time.
- Counteractant theory: reactive compounds bond with amines, mercaptans and organic acids to form odourless, non-volatile complexes.
- Three-tier deodoriser application: inline, post-extraction while damp, and sub-surface injection for contamination through the backing.
- Truckmount heat deactivates live enzyme cultures.
- Olefin is hydrophobic, so liquids spread rather than absorb and the contamination zone widens faster than on nylon.
- UV mapping as a systematic pre-treatment diagnostic rather than a sales demo.
- Tannin stains need acid-side chemistry because alkaline presprays set them and trigger browning.
- Checking for Crypton or Scotchgard-protected fabrics before spraying.
- Verifying rinse completion by feel on dry carpet and with a pH meter on extracted water.
